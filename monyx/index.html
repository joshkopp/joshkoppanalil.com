Why we built this

Most wallets will let you send to a typo.

That's the whole story. No second look, no "wait, is this the same person?" A friend of ours almost paid the wrong person because two addresses looked the same at both ends. There's no undo on that, which still sits with us.

The look-alike address thing is called address poisoning: a close copy shows up in your history so you paste the wrong one. A token with a copied logo is another one. And people get rushed. We've all been rushed.

What we wanted

The careful part should happen before you hit send, in regular sentences. Not afterwards, in a block explorer.

Dollars, not tokens

A dollar stays a dollar. And there's no fee.

Your balance is in USDC, a digital dollar, so it doesn't swing around like other crypto. Every send also needs a tiny network fee, normally paid in a token you'd have to go buy first. We pay it for you. You never see it, and you never need to own anything but dollars.

An assistant that can't spend

Let Grok Bot handle the asking. You keep the sending.

Connect Grok Bot with a key from your Me tab and it can ask friends for rent, check who owes you, and draft payments. Drafts wait on your home screen under "Waiting for your OK." The key has no way to move money on its own. Every draft goes through the same preview and check as a payment you start yourself.

How this started

We got into crypto the usual student way.

A little late, a little lost, and suddenly responsible for money in apps that assumed we already knew the rules. Then we watched friends almost mess it up. Look-alike addresses. Tokens that weren't what they said. A group chat that made a send feel urgent.

So we built Monyx at the UBC 2026 hackathon. The rest of the story, and the two of us.

Try the demo.

It runs on Solana devnet, so the balances are play money. We built it so we can add more chains later.
